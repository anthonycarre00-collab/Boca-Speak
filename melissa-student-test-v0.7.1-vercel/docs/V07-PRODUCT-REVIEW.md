# Melissa v0.7 — Product Review Notes

## Competitive direction

Current pronunciation apps already cover AI pronunciation scoring, personalized sound paths, structured speaking practice and real-world scenarios. Melissa should not try to be a smaller copy of those systems.

The v0.7 product boundary is therefore:

- Spanish-first navigation and explanation.
- A deliberately human-readable pronunciation bridge rather than IPA.
- Real-life phrases rather than isolated drills only.
- Record / listen / compare without forcing a score.
- Teacher-friendly observation and future content editing.
- Light motivation through progress, repetition, streaks and small achievements.
- No competitive league system.

## Gamification decision

Gamification earns its place only when it points back to useful behaviour. Melissa therefore rewards:

- completing a real practice;
- hitting the three-practice daily target;
- returning on another day;
- exploring more material;
- identifying bridges that genuinely help.

It intentionally does not reward raw grinding indefinitely. Points stop increasing after the daily target, which keeps the reward loop attached to the learning routine rather than to button pressing.

## Pronunciation bridge decision

The earlier convention of using `z` as a cue for English TH was rejected for the Colombian-first prototype. Colombian and most Latin American Spanish varieties use seseo, so `z` is normally heard as /s/. Melissa v0.7 uses:

- `s̱` — a visual reminder for voiceless TH: tongue between the teeth, no voice;
- `ḏ` — a visual reminder for voiced TH: tongue between the teeth, with voice.

This is intentionally a teaching notation, not an IPA transcription.

## Features held back

- AI pronunciation scoring.
- AI conversational tutor.
- social leaderboards.
- XP economy with endless grinding.
- complex avatar systems.
- paid-gating logic.
- full cloud accounts / classroom sync.

Those can be evaluated later only if student testing shows a clear need.
